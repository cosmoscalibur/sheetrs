### Changed

- The cells of the linter's workbook are kept in a `Grid<Cell>` (`sheetrs_core::reader::Grid`: sorted columns per row with the offset of each row) instead of a hash map, and the compiler's model keeps its cells in the same store; both are filled through the reader's `CellSink`, so a format's cells are read by one method (`read_sheets_into`) and `read_sheets` is provided. Cells are visited in row order. The output of the linter is byte-identical.
- INT401–403 keep each cell as 12 bytes with its formula pattern interned (they kept the pattern of every formula cell, and copied it again by row and by column), and REF309 asks the sheets for the cells instead of copying every position into a set. Peak memory of the linter with all rules and the human output: 49 MB workbook (6.6 M cells) 1,513 MB → 968 MB and 31 s → 16 s; Delgado 527 → 386 MB; 1.2 M formulas 933 → 649 MB. Alone, the read takes 1,008 → 542 MB (49 MB workbook), 277 → 178 MB (Delgado). With the streamed JSON output, Delgado goes from 3.4 GB to 386 MB.

- **API:** a `Cell` no longer holds its row and column (the sheet's `Grid` has them), and its `num_fmt` is a one-pointer `NumFmt` shared by the cells that have it (`Option<Arc<str>>` before), so a cell is 56 bytes instead of 72. `Grid::iter` and `Sheet::cells.iter()` give `((row, col), &Cell)`; `CellSink::cell(sheet, (row, col), cell)` and `WalkerRule::on_cell(sheet, (row, col), cell, …)` and `LinterContext::formula_upper(sheet, (row, col), cell)` take the position. INT401–403 read the sheet at its end and keep only the rows and columns with enough formulas to report anything, and VUL601 groups the sheet's own formula text; neither keeps a copy of the cells. Linter peak, all rules, human output: 49 MB workbook (6.6 M cells) 1,513 MB → 604 MB (31 s → 15 s); Delgado 527 → 359 MB; 1.2 M formulas 927 → 530 MB; read alone 1,008 → 441 MB. The output is byte-identical (MD5 of the human and JSON output of four workbooks) and the compiler's programs are identical.
